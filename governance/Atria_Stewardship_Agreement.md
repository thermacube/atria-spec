# **Atria Stewardship Agreement**

*(Private / Internal)*

## **Purpose**

This document defines the stewardship relationship between **Atria**, **Thermacube**, and **the primary steward (Matt)**. Its purpose is to:

- protect the steward from overload and identity entanglement,

- ensure the long-term health and integrity of the Atria project,

- clarify authority and boundaries,

- and provide a clear reference point when pressure, ambiguity, or drift arises.

This agreement is not legal.  
It is **normative and operational**.

## **1. Steward Role and Authority**

### **1.1 Stewardship Authority**

The primary steward is the **final authority** on all matters related to:

- Atria’s core architecture

- Atria’s philosophical and technical kernel

- Atria’s engineering specifications

- What Atria *is* and *is not  
  *

This authority is **not shared**, delegated, or subject to consensus.

Disagreement does not obligate change.

### **1.2 Relationship to the Specifications**

The Atria engineering specifications (0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3 and bridge specs) are the **non-negotiable contract** for Atria’s behavior.

The steward’s role is to:

- steward the coherence of these specs,

- evolve them carefully,

- and prevent erosion of their intent.

Requests that violate the kernel are resolved by **forking**, not accommodation.

## **2. Time, Rhythm, and Availability**

### **2.1 Normal Engagement**

In a healthy season, stewardship engagement is:

- **5–8 hours per week  
  **

- Primarily **early morning hours** (approximately 5–7 or 5–8am)

- Additional time is **opportunistic**, not expected

Availability beyond this window varies by:

- Thermacube business needs

- family

- ministry

- life context

No expectation of immediate response exists.

### **2.2 Hard Boundaries**

The steward is **not available** for:

- on-call support

- emergency debugging

- real-time operational response

- reactive availability tied to others’ timelines

Delayed responses are normal and acceptable.

## **3. Modes of Work**

### **3.1 Energizing Work (Primary)**

The steward’s healthy contribution modes are:

- architectural design

- systems reasoning

- specification writing (AI-assisted)

- low-volume teaching (1–2 engagements per month)

- low-volume deep technical conversations (1–2 per month)

- prototyping

- AI-assisted coding

These activities are the *intended* form of involvement.

### **3.2 Draining Work (Avoided)**

The steward does **not** routinely perform:

- repeated explanations of the same concepts

- emotional regulation of frustrated users or admins

- support queues or ticket handling

- long email threads

- operational troubleshooting under time pressure

If these activities begin to dominate, stewardship is considered **out of bounds**.

## **4. Requests, Pressure, and Change Management**

### **4.1 Nature of Requests**

All requests—whether from Thermacube, partners, or institutions—are treated as:

> **Requests, never demands.**

A request does not imply:

- a real constraint,

- a design flaw,

- or an obligation to act.

All requests are evaluated independently against the kernel.

### **4.2 Violations of Core Principles**

If a requested change violates Atria’s principles:

- the system is **not altered**,

- Thermacube does **not** produce a special version,

- the requesting party is invited to **fork the system**.

Forking is treated as a **valid and healthy outcome**, with no ill will.

## **5. Emotional Safety and Early Warning Signals**

### **5.1 Warning Signs**

The following are explicit indicators that stewardship has become unhealthy:

- overfunctioning

- people-pleasing

- hyper-vigilance around issues

- erosion of time boundaries

- internal pressure to compromise core principles to satisfy others

These are **signals**, not failures.

### **5.2 Default Response to Warning Signs**

When warning signs appear, the default response is to:

1.  Pause new commitments

2.  Delegate or disengage from the source of pressure

3.  Reduce scope

4.  Resume new partner work **only after clarity returns  
    **

This response is **internal and private** and does not require explanation or justification.

## **6. Continuity and Absence**

### **6.1 Planned or Unplanned Absence**

If the steward is unavailable for an extended period (up to 6 months):

Acceptable outcomes include:

- Thermacube continues operating Atria

- Development slows or pauses

- Only bug fixes are applied

- Existing partners self-support

- Prospective partners wait or use asynchronous training

- No one is “waiting” on the steward

### **6.2 Forking Expectations**

Forking is expected **only when genuinely needed**.

Forking due to short-term inactivity or impatience is discouraged but tolerated.

A stable system should remain viable for **at least one year** with only bug fixes.

## **7. Relationship to Thermacube**

### **7.1 Steward vs Company**

The steward is **not equivalent to Thermacube**.

- Thermacube may operate, deploy, train, and consult

- The steward is not obligated to be the operational face of the system

- Other Thermacube staff may assume responsibilities that do not require architectural authority

### **7.2 Delegation**

The steward explicitly supports delegation of:

- training delivery

- architecture reviews

- implementation assistance

- partner communication

The steward does **not** delegate:

- architectural authority

- kernel definition

- philosophical direction

## **8. Definition of a Personal Win**

Stewardship is considered successful if:

- Atria demonstrates the steward’s full architectural capability

- Institutions gain autonomy and integrity

- Others succeed without requiring the steward’s constant presence

- The steward remains at peace, not overextended

- The work remains life-giving rather than identity-bearing

## **9. Final Statement**

Atria exists to express a way of thinking about learning execution that values:

- correctness

- auditability

- autonomy

- and integrity

Stewardship is successful when these values persist **without requiring the steward to sacrifice personal boundaries, emotional energy, or freedom**.
